01 / The problem

From 3D images to a lineage.

Biohub’s Cell Tracking During Development challenge followed cell nuclei through time in three-dimensional microscopy of zebrafish embryos. The output was a graph: detected cells as nodes, temporal connections as edges, and branches where a parent divided into two daughter cells.

A plausible-looking detection in each frame was only part of the task. A missed cell could break a trajectory. Two nearby cells could exchange identities. A false division could create a lineage that never happened.

What the competition measured

The score combined adjusted edge Jaccard + 0.1 × division Jaccard. Predicted and reference nodes were matched within a 7 μm tolerance. That made the quality of connections—and the treatment of divisions—central to the result.

02 / The approach

Detect locally.
Connect globally.

I built on pretrained detection and association components, then worked on how their outputs became coherent tracks. The pipeline combined learned scores with explicit constraints and post-processing checks.

  1. Find cell-center candidatesA TemporalUNet3D detector proposed nuclei from the 3D image sequence. Those detections formed the candidates the tracker could connect.
  2. Score possible connectionsA transformer used 33-dimensional point features and bidirectional cross-attention between adjacent frames. It produced pairwise link scores and scores for parent–daughter division configurations.
  3. Select a consistent graphAn integer linear program selected links using appearance, disappearance, and division costs. Constraints limited a cell to one parent and at most two children.
  4. Check the difficult transitionsMotion-based relinking, candidate readmission, and gap filling addressed broken connections. Additional center-evidence and geometric checks restricted implausible divisions and gap links.

This separation mattered: a model could rank individual links, while the optimization stage enforced relationships across the graph. Neither stage alone settled every ambiguous case.

03 / Technical decisions

The hard cases
were between frames.

Problem How I addressed it
Movement and broken links Motion-based relinking reconsidered associations when the original connection was inconsistent with the surrounding trajectory.
Missing detections Readmission and gap-filling logic gave the tracker a way to recover candidates and reconnect interrupted tracks. These extensions needed separate validation from the main detector.
False divisions A DeepCenter center-prior model checked for supporting image evidence. Parent–child distance, sister distance, and division-rate caps added geometric and frequency constraints.
Premature track endings The disappearance cost in the optimization controlled the trade-off between ending a trajectory and preserving a candidate link.

These were coupled decisions. Making it easier to keep a track alive could also keep a wrong association alive. Adding a missing point could repair one lineage while introducing a false branch elsewhere.

04 / Evaluation

A local improvement
needs a second test.

I inspected edge and division errors separately and compared candidate outputs at the movie and embryo level. Replays of existing detections were useful for screening changes to the association stage, but did not replace running the complete pipeline.

The experiment record also exposed a validation pitfall: some movies used to assess an additional learned ranker overlapped that ranker’s training data. Improvements on those movies could not establish generalization. Other changes behaved differently across embryo groups, and readmission and gap filling did not have the same independent support as the better-tested components.

The boundary of the evidence

The technical discussion describes the pipeline and changes developed during the project. It does not assign the final score to an individual component or treat every local gain as an improvement on unseen data.

The verified competition outcome was 51st of 3,947 teams, with a silver medal and a final private score of 0.93486.

05 / What I took away

The graph is
the prediction.

Getting the cells right is not enough. Their connections have to make sense together.

This project made me think about machine learning as a system of interacting decisions. Detection, association, optimization, and post-processing each had a job—and each could undermine the others.

My strongest lesson was about evaluation: check the provenance of every trained component, separate tuning from confirmation, and test complete sequences. A validation split is only independent if it is independent of the entire pipeline.
